01

What the incubator does

Two audiences, one face: a development finance officer reading a quarterly report, and a business owner deciding whether to walk into the centre.

LEEMA Incubation NPC is a township enterprise incubator in Mogwase, North West. We work with small enterprises across the North West, Gauteng and the Northern Cape, funded through Seda and SEDFA.

An enterprise arrives at the centre with a business, not a business plan. We take one profile — what it sells, who it sells to, where it trades, and what it is trying to reach — and place it on one of four footings. The footing decides what the centre owes it: accredited training, funding readiness, equipment, suppliers, or a route to a customer it cannot reach on its own.

Nothing here is a standard package. Each of the nine enterprises in the current cohort named its own list of what it wants from the centre, and those lists are what we work.

LEEMA Incubation NPC is the parent, and it is the name used in registration, banking, contracts, funder correspondence and PFMA compliance statements. Leema Township Incubator is the programme, and it is the name on everything public-facing.

A note on the counting

Figures are counted from the profiles

Every number on this page is computed from the nine intake records in the data file behind it, so the charts and the profiles cannot drift apart. Jobs are the exception worth naming: fifteen are projected across the cohort — ten at Reve P Catering once the resort is operating, five at Far Out Excellent. Both are projections. Neither is counted as a job created.

Each enterprise's funding gap, cash-flow position and stated weakness stay out of the public record. They were submitted to the incubator and its funder. They appear here in aggregate, which is the form in which they argue for funding without naming which business is short of it.

05

How it works

Four steps from a phone call to a line in a funder report.

How the pipeline is tracked

The centre runs a process-mining system over its own intake and support records — the AREFA/VID3 system, described in full in the next section. Stripped of the terminology, it does four things a paper file cannot.

It maps what actually happens. Every profile, compliance check and support action leaves a timestamp, so the route an enterprise takes through the centre is drawn from the record rather than from memory.

It finds where enterprises get stuck. Waiting times between steps are measured, so a delay in funding approval or a compliance item sitting unclosed shows up as a number rather than as a complaint.

It reads the two directions together. What the enterprises report from the market — cash flow, capital, route to a customer — is set against what the funders require, so the support the centre gives is aimed at the constraint the businesses actually named.

It matches the cohort to itself. Nine enterprises across seven sectors buy from and sell to each other. The one with the ICT stock and the one that needs a website are in the same list.

06

AREFA

The system behind section 05, on the centre's own hardware. It is the instrument the pipeline figures are drawn with — and, like every other figure on this site, what it claims about itself is measured, fingerprinted and printed rather than asserted.

What it is for

Four questions, asked of the cohort

A scripted exchange, so that what AREFA is for can be read without it installed. The questions are fixed and the prose is written — but every figure in the answers is counted from the nine intake records at the moment you click, the same records section 02 is drawn from.

Scripted demo Not a live model · figures counted from the nine records

This is a staged exchange, not AREFA answering. The four questions are the only four it takes, and the shape of each reply is written in the page source. What is not staged is the arithmetic: every count above is tallied from the cohort data when you press the button, so the demo cannot say something the profiles do not.

AREFA is not running on this page, and cannot be. It runs on the centre's own machine, and the transfer reports record no network exposure and none authorised.

The chain it writes about itself

Every gate, in order, including the one still open

Each gate re-measures the system and writes a report with the fingerprints it took. A gate that has not run is written as open. Nothing below is a plan; it is the reading.

Reported empty rather than estimated

What has not happened yet

The transfer of this system to an intern at the centre is part-run, not finished. The rows above say so. They will say something else when the gate that changes them has passed, and not before.

Fingerprints

Check the claim, do not take it

These are SHA-256 digests, not secrets. They are printed at full length so that anyone holding a copy of the system can recompute them and see for themselves whether they hold.

The boundary

What is never carried with it

The transfer contract names the exclusions before anything is packed, and the first gate checks them. SSH keys, browser profiles and cookies, shell history, cloud credentials, authentication configuration, password stores, and unrelated personal files and application state are out of scope by contract, not by care taken on the day.

The same rule runs the other way. Historical audit files are immutable: they are never rewritten to make a path tidier for transfer, because a record edited for convenience is no longer a record. Absolute paths and the operator's home directory are not published on this page for the same reason they are not published for the cohort — they identify a person.

Why it sits on this site

Skills development, in its most literal form

Measure 01 is SMME skills development. The whole working system — runtime, launcher, services, audit timers, project and evidence chain, and the model itself — is being reproduced for an intern at the centre, intact and checkable, rather than described in a training slide. When the last gate passes, that is one training intervention that can be verified by digest.

Artwork required AREFA mark · the continent drawn as a printed-circuit board, gold traces on ink, and the baobab plate at dusk with signal running through the grass. Both need keying to transparency and an ink and a white version before they go on the site, held the same way the LEEMA mark is held.
